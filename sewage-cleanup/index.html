A sewage backup is one of the most hazardous situations a property owner can face. Anyone who comes in contact with contaminated material is exposed to parasites, bacteria and viruses, among them giardia, E. coli, salmonella and hepatitis, all carried in raw sewage and all dangerous. When it happens in Rowlett, Rowlett Restoration is available 24/7 with technicians trained for this work, proper protective gear and disinfectants registered with the EPA, everything needed for a safe decontamination.

Water damage comes in categories, and sewage sits in the worst one: Category 3, called black water, the highest level of contamination and danger. This classification requires the most stringent cleanup protocols, including full personal protective equipment, containment barriers, removal of all porous materials that contacted sewage and thorough disinfection of every remaining surface. For Category 3 water damage the reference is IICRC S500, and our crews follow it to bring the building back to safe occupancy.

We clear the standing sewage first, then pull contaminated water out of structural materials, remove carpet, padding, insulation, drywall and any other porous item that was in contact with sewage for proper disposal, disinfect each hard surface, treat with antimicrobial products and finish with structural drying, which prevents secondary mold. Lingering sewage odor is eliminated with thermal fogging and industrial hydroxyl generators, which we run in the affected area.

The Benefits of Professional Sewage Cleanup

Sewage cleanup is not something any homeowner should attempt. The biological hazards in raw sewage require specialized training, proper personal protective equipment (Tyvek suits, respirators, protective gloves and boots) and EPA-registered disinfection products that are not available to consumers. Exposure to sewage pathogens can cause serious illness, including gastrointestinal disease, hepatitis, respiratory infections and skin infections.

Disposal matters as well, since everything the sewage contaminated has to be handled as biohazard waste. Simply placing sewage-contaminated drywall, carpet and insulation in your regular trash is both illegal and hazardous to sanitation workers and the community. Licensed biohazard disposal facilities take this waste from certified restoration firms, which keep the required protocols in place.

Maintenance Tips for Sewage Cleanup

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Food waste, oils and grease block pipes, so put them in the trash and not the sink
  • Have your sewer line inspected with a camera every 2 to 3 years to catch potential problems
  • In an emergency a plumber needs the cleanout, so learn where yours is and keep it in good shape
  • Toilet paper is the only thing to flush, and that rules out wipes sold as flushable, which still clog
  • A backwater valve at the sewer connection stops the city system from backing up into your home
  • Keep downspouts and sump pump discharge out of the sanitary sewer so it does not overload in heavy rain

Warning Signs for Sewage Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Toilets or drains that gurgle whenever you run water somewhere else
  • A rotten egg or sewer smell from the drains or the lowest floor of the home
  • Multiple drains in your home backing up at the same time
  • Carpet or flooring contaminated when a toilet overflowed with solid waste
  • Family members falling ill, especially with gastrointestinal trouble, for no obvious reason
  • Foul-smelling moisture showing up on flooring or along the walls
  • Dark residue from sewage left on the basement floor or ringing a drain
