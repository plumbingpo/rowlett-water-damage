Sump Pump Failure Cleanup Help in Rowlett

A sump pump has one job, and it tends to fail at the moment it is needed most. Heavy rain raises the water table, the pit fills, and then the power goes out, the float sticks or the pump cannot keep up. From there it tops the edge of the basin and runs out over the slab. Until pumping resumes, the water continues to rise.

This water is not clean. Soil, silt, bacteria and the contents of the drain tile travel with it, so our team rates it no better than Category 2, and Category 3 when it has stood for a while or sewage is involved. For the job, this means soaked porous items such as drywall and carpet pad are usually discarded, and what stays gets washed and disinfected first, then dried.

Your First Steps

  1. Keep Out of the BasementNever wade into basement water before the power has been cut. The furnace, wall outlets or the cord of the pump itself could be in contact with it.
  2. Shut Off Basement PowerIf the breaker panel can be reached while you stand somewhere dry, switch off the circuits for the basement. If the panel is in the water, call the utility.
  3. Check the Pump SafelyA tripped outlet, a loose plug or a float wedged against the basin wall are the things to look for. Reaching into the pit or the water is not safe.
  4. Treat the Water as ContaminatedWear rubber boots and gloves if you must go down, keep kids and pets upstairs, and wash your hands after touching anything wet.
  5. Move Valuables and CallCarry dry valuables upstairs, photograph the water level and the pump, then call us. Waiting for it to recede without help is a mistake.
Coverage

What About Insurance?

Few water claims confuse homeowners as much as this one. A standard homeowners policy usually excludes water that backs up or overflows from a sump, and flood insurance is a separate policy with its own rules. Unless you bought an endorsement for sump overflow or water backup there is often no coverage, and such endorsements are capped. Your declarations page will list it, or your agent can tell you. We record the pump failure, the water level and the damage so your claim file is complete.

Our Process

Our Sump Pump Failure Cleanup Process

1

Pump Out the Basement

We bring our own pumps and extractors, so nothing depends on your failed sump. Standing water and silt are removed from the floor, the pit area and any low spots.

2

Remove Contaminated Materials

Our team wears protective gear to remove wet drywall, insulation, carpet and pad reached by groundwater, then bags and hauls it off, leaving bare framing and concrete.

3

Wash and Disinfect

An antimicrobial product goes on after the concrete, the studs and any kept belongings have been washed. Doing it ahead of drying keeps the air movers from spreading contaminants through the basement.

4

Dry and Verify

Dehumidifiers and air movers run until moisture meter readings on the framing and concrete return to normal, typically 3 to 5 days. Before we pull our equipment, the pit should have a pump that works.

The Usual Culprits

How It Usually Starts

  • Gravel, silt or debris in the pit jamming the impeller or clogging the intake.
  • More water flowing into the pit than an undersized pump can move.
  • No backup battery to keep the pump going once storm winds knock out power.
  • The pump being unplugged by accident or sitting on an outlet that tripped.
  • Stuck or snagged float switches, which never tell the pump to start.
If It Is Left

Where the Damage Spreads

Wall Framing

The wood plate at the base of each wall sits right on the slab and soaks up water first. Once the drywall is off, the plates and studs are washed, disinfected and dried.

Basement Floor Coverings

Groundwater-soaked carpet and padding come out, as cleaning cannot make them safe. Silty water also gets caught below vinyl plank and laminate, which normally means lifting them.

Outlets and Wiring

Wall outlets near the floor are sometimes submerged in a sump flood. Any outlet or wiring that got wet should be checked by an electrician before the circuit is turned back on.

Drywall and Wall Insulation

Drywall and insulation wick dirty water upward from the slab, well above the visible water line. Because the water is contaminated, the wet lower section is cut out, not dried.
