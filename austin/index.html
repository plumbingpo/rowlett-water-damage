Simple Ways to Keep an Austin Home Dry

Houses tell you when water is getting where it should not. The signs are easy to miss if you do not know them, so here is what our technicians look for in Austin homes. Paint that bubbles or peels, a brown ring on a ceiling, baseboards pulling away from the wall, and flooring that cups or lifts all mean moisture behind the surface. Add a stale odor in an enclosed space, a loose toilet, or a faucet that keeps dripping.

Taken singly they are minor, though every one of them gets more expensive with time. Re-caulk tubs and showers when the bead cracks, since water behind tile does damage you will not see for months. Test a sump pump several times a year by filling the pit until the float trips and the motor runs. In basements and garages, shelving and plastic tubs keep belongings clear of the floor.

Good upkeep lowers the odds, but it cannot remove them. What it does is make them rarer and smaller, and that matters when the alternative is days of drying equipment in your Austin living room.

What Usually Goes Wrong in Austin Homes

Not every water problem announces itself with a flooded floor. Here is what homeowners around Austin phone us about most, along with what usually sits behind each one.

Hidden leaks: Small drips from a worn fitting or a tiny hole in a pipe may go unnoticed behind drywall for a long time. Watch for a water bill that climbs for no reason, soft spots on the floor, and paint that blisters. Thermal imaging and moisture readings let us map how far it spread before anything gets cut open. Left alone, constant dampness rots framing and feeds mold, so a minor drip is still worth fixing now.

Sewage: A blocked sewer line sends waste back into the house, usually at the lowest shower, toilet or floor drain. It is typically caused by roots in the line, built-up grease or a municipal sewer overwhelmed during a downpour. This is Category 3 water, which carries bacteria and should not be handled without protective gear. Porous items it touches, such as carpet and pad, generally have to be removed.

Storms: Hard rain can outrun gutters and yard drains, then pool against the house. It enters at door thresholds, low windows and the garage, wherever the ground slopes toward the building. Because surface water carries dirt and chemicals from the ground it passed over, it is handled as unsanitary. A big enough storm can cause it at any time of year, and Austin properties are no exception.

Crawlspace: The space beneath a raised floor is easy to forget, which lets dampness go unchecked for years. Humidity stays high down there when a pipe drips, the ground cover is damaged or water pools after rain. You may notice cupped floorboards, a stale smell in the living area or insulation hanging loose. We pump out what has collected, dry the joists and subfloor, and show you where the moisture is coming from.

Condensate: Cooling equipment produces a steady stream of condensation whenever it runs in damp weather. That water should leave through a small drain line, which clogs easily with algae and dust. A blockage makes the drip pan spill over, wetting the ceiling or floor near the indoor unit. Most owners learn of it from a ceiling stain or drooping drywall, so have the drain line cleared once a year.

What to Do Before Our Austin Crew Arrives

What you do before help reaches your Austin address has a real effect on how the job goes. Safety comes before saving anything. If water is near outlets, cords or the electrical panel, stay out of it, and switch off power to that area only if you can reach the breaker without stepping in water. Water that came up from a drain or sewer line should be left completely alone.

Next, stop the source. Close the valve at the fixture if there is one, or the main shutoff for the house. After that, carry light items such as rugs, documents, electronics and chairs somewhere dry. Put foil or plastic under the legs of heavy furniture so the wood does not stain the carpet.

Before mopping, record the damage on your phone, since an insurance company will ask what it looked like at the start. Mop or towel up small amounts of clean water if it is safe, and leave the rest to the crew heading to Austin.
